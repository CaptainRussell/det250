        Cadre Proctor Tools

        For whoever runs the assessment

        What to have ready

        • An AED on site, inside the building or within 100 feet.
        • Each cadet's Fitness Screening Questionnaire (AF Form 4446A), done first on the day.
        • One AF Form 4446 scorecard per member.
        • The run course measured and certified with a measuring wheel. The track layout is under Marking the Track.
        • A current sports physical for every cadet testing.
        • Stopwatches for the run and the plank, and a tape for height and waist.
        • Anyone on the HAMR: the tally sheets printed and the pacing audio saved to a speaker.
        • The verbal instructions to read, under On the Day.

        A working checklist built from the rules below, not a regulation.

        An AED has to be there

        Official assessments and PT are run with an automated external defibrillator present, inside the building or within 100 feet, and it stays on site until everyone has left the area. If there is no AED, the assessment does not run.

        AFROTCI 36-2011 V3, para 10.6.2, citing DAFMAN 36-2905

        Before the assessment can be official

        The 2 mile run course has to be measured and certified with a measuring wheel first, or in coordination with the university. A sports physical is valid for one calendar year from the date of examination.

        AFROTC Guidance Memorandum 2026-01, paras 1.2.5.3.1 and 3.13.7; NOTACC CY26-092

        Where to put each group's start and finish lines, measured back from the painted finish. Set the distance and the track, then print it for whoever is out with the wheel.

        How the day runs

        The Fitness Screening Questionnaire is completed before anything else, and may be re-accomplished if something has changed since. On a cadet's FSQ, questions 2 and 3 do not apply: cadets are not sickle cell tested until their first duty station. All three physical components are done inside a 3 hour window on the same day. Up to a 3 minute warm-up is allowed before the run, the HAMR or the walk.

        Body composition may be measured separately from the rest, by no more than 5 duty days. Outside that window the assessment counts as a did-not-finish and has to be done again.

        DAFMAN 36-2905 paras 3.15.9, 3.4.3 and Attachment 2. AFROTC Guidance Memorandum 2026-01 (paras 3.4.5.1 and 3.4.5.2) lets a cadet choose to measure body composition separately inside those 5 duty days, at a time and place the detachment commander publishes.

        The verbal instructions

        Read word for word before each component. Each one opens the full script from DAFMAN 36-2905 Attachment 2, with the recording where there is one. The AFROTC events are marked.

        After an Unsatisfactory

        Follow AFROTCI 36-2011 V3 for what a failed assessment costs and how many attempts there are (Table 5.4, and para 17.2.3.1 for the commissioning QFR). The old 42 day wait before a retest is gone.

        The 42 day rule was 2023 AFROTC Supplement para 5.5.1.4, deleted by AFROTC Guidance Memorandum 2026-01 on 30 October 2026.

        If you are on a medical restriction

        Cadets are deferred from the assessment while on Medical Restriction Status. On release there is a 42 day reconditioning period, and you cannot be made to test during it, though you may choose to, in which case the assessment counts. The requirement is to pass no later than the 60th calendar day after removal from MRS.

        AFROTCI 36-2011 V3, paras 8.7.2 and 8.7.2.1

        A summary, not a substitute. AFROTC Guidance Memorandum 2026-01 (30 October 2026, at the front of the AFROTC Supplement) wins over other AFROTC publications where they differ; then NOTACC CY26-092, AFROTCI 36-2011 V3 and DAFMAN 36-2905. The manuals are in References, below.
