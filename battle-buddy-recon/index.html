Step 2 · Expectations

Find what an adversary would find.

The wheel will pair you with a random cadet. You get 30 minutes to find everything you can about them using open sources only. Then you brief what you found. Your partner is doing the same to you.

~5 minPairing

The wheel draws two cadets at a time. Each of you researches the other.

30 minRecon

Open-source research on your partner. Work alone and quietly. Don't tip them off.

~44 minBrief-out

2 minutes per cadet. Your partner then confirms or corrects what you found.

TonightLock down

Fix what was found on you: privacy settings, old posts, geotags, security answers.

Why we do this. Criminals, scammers, and foreign intelligence services build profiles on service members exactly this way. They piece together small public details until they have a person's location, routine, and leverage. If a classmate can find it in 30 minutes, so can they.

What to look for

IdentityFull name, photos, usernames reused across platforms, email handles
LocationHometown, home or dorm area, places they check in or tag
Pattern of lifeGym times, run routes, work or class schedule, weekly habits
RelationshipsFamily, close friends, significant other, who they're always tagged with
Security answersPet names, high school, mascot, first car, mother's maiden name
AffiliationUnit, school, team, job, upcoming travel, anything military

Bonus credit for aggregation: a conclusion you reached by combining two or more harmless details, like a gym check-in plus a tagged coffee shop that narrows down where someone lives.

Rules of engagement

Allowed

  • Search engines and reverse image search
  • Public social media profiles, posts, and comments
  • Public news articles, team rosters, published results
  • Details visible in the background of public photos

Off limits

  • Friend or follow requests, DMs, or contacting their friends or family
  • Fake accounts, pretexting, or asking anyone about your partner
  • Paid people-search or data-broker sites
  • Logging into anything, guessing passwords or security answers
  • Following or watching your partner in person

Find it, don't record it

  • Medical or health information
  • Religion, sexual orientation, politics
  • Financial details, SSN, ID numbers
  • Exact street addresses and phone numbers

Write the category only, e.g. "found exact home address." The lesson is that it was findable.

Brief-out standard

  1. Exposure level. Low, medium, or high, in one sentence.
  2. Top findings. Your 3 most significant, by category.
  3. How you found them. Source type and any aggregation chain.
  4. So what. How an adversary could use it.
  5. One fix. The single change your partner should make first.

Your partner gets the last word: they confirm, correct, or say "redact," and you move on.

Conduct

  • Everything you find stays in this room. No screenshots, no sharing, no bringing it up later.
  • Hand your notes to your partner at the end. They keep or shred them.
  • Misuse of anything found is an honor and conduct violation.
  • Opting out is fine and needs no reason. See the instructor before pairing.
